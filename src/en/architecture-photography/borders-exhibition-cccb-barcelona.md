## Exhibition Design Photography: “Borders” at CCCB Barcelona

This cultural interior photography series documents the spatial design and scenography of the **“Borders” exhibition** at the **[CCCB](https://www.cccb.org/en) (Centre de Cultura Contemporània de Barcelona) in Barcelona’s** historic El Raval district. The CCCB is globally recognised for its thought-provoking, multidisciplinary cultural programming.

The photoshoot focuses on how the temporary architecture and exhibition designed by [EMBA, Enric Massip-Bosch Arquitectes](https://www.emba.cat) can transform a grand museum hall, guiding visitors through a dense, emotionally charged, and highly conceptual narrative.

### Documenting Scenography, Spatial Boundaries, and Dramatic Light

Photographing major thematic museum exhibitions requires acute sensitivity to spatial flow, graphic curation, and specialised atmospheric lighting.

* **The Architecture of Limits:** Mirroring the exhibition’s core theme, the scenography uses a complex network of dark partition walls, sudden spatial compressions, and open thresholds. The photography captures this intentional layout through deep vanishing points, showing how the structures separate distinct sub-themes while creating an ongoing visual dialogue across the gallery floor.
* **Balancing Multimedia and Graphic Elements:** The exhibition blends large-format maps, historical text blocks, and immersive video projection rooms. The camera exposures are precisely metered to balance these disparate light sources—capturing the crisp legibility of backlit informational panels while preserving the rich, shadow-heavy ambience of the surrounding installation spaces.
* **Thematic Spotlight Tracking:** The compositions highlight the specialised overhead track lighting, showing how focused beams of light isolate fine art pieces and historical documents, turning them into dramatic focal points within a dark, contemplative void.

### Technical Accuracy in Low-Light Museum Portfolios

Documenting immersive, low-light cultural installations brings considerable technical challenges for an architectural photographer. Every frame in this gallery is composed with strict **orthogonal alignment**, keeping vertical lines true across temporary partition structures and structural columns.

By applying advanced exposure bracketing and careful colour balance in line with the museum’s lighting design, the photography prevents muddiness in deep shadows and preserves the tone of the multimedia displays. This portfolio piece serves as a premium visual archive, preserving the complex spatial layout and ephemeral craftsmanship of a world-class Barcelona exhibition rollout.
